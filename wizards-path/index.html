How it works

Most chess learning drowns you in strategies. This path starts with you: how you think, what you fear, where you shine. Every lesson trains a chess skill and a character muscle together.

  • For any age. A six-year-old and a sixty-year-old walk the same path. Each lesson includes notes for both.
  • For players past the basics. You know how the pieces move and the common ideas. We build from there.
  • Every lesson is complete in itself. Start with any lesson. The numbered order is the recommended path.
  • One lesson = 60 minutes. Centre (3) · Idea (7–10) · Train (15–20) · Play (20–25) · Reflect (7–10).
  • Stories from the Mahabharata. Each lesson opens with a short retelling of an episode from the epic, then turns it into a chess lesson. They are brief retellings in our own words; the epic has many versions, so read it in a translation you trust.
  • Your style leads. After Lesson 1, every lesson shows your personality's version first.
  • Go deeper, your way. Each lesson ends with search keywords for books, videos and puzzle sets.

The four guides

Your Board Self in Lesson 1 is tied to a figure of the epic: The Storm (Bhima), The Fortress (Bhishma), The Architect (Drona) and The Fox (Krishna's art of timing). Arjuna, the learner, walks through all four. Search keywords for the stories are at the end of each lesson. For first reading: The Mahabharata by R. K. Narayan, or the Amar Chitra Katha comics for younger readers.

The daily rhythm

Small, daily and complete beats big and occasional.

  1. Centre. Breathe. Set an intention.
  2. Idea. One clear lesson, in plain words.
  3. Train. Short drills and honest self-checks.
  4. Play. A real game with one focus.
  5. Reflect. Write what you learned about the game and about yourself.
