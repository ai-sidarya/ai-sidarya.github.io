From the Mahabharata · Indraprastha

The Pandavas were given only a rough, wild stretch of land to rule. Many would have complained. Instead, with Krishna's guidance and the skilled builder Maya, they cleared the forest, planned the streets and raised the splendid city of Indraprastha.

It did not appear in a day. It was built plan by plan, stone by stone.

The chess lesson: a good position is built the same way. Find the weakest piece, give it a job, and keep improving, one patient move at a time.

1 · Centre

3 min

The builder does not hurry the stone. The builder lays it right.

  1. Three slow breaths.
  2. Picture a wild field. Now picture it as a city. What would be the first step?
  3. Say: "I will build, not rush."

2 · The Idea

10 min

At your level, most games are decided by whoever has a plan when the position is quiet. A plan is not a calculation. It is a sentence: "I want to ______ by ______." Write that sentence at move 10, move 20 and move 30 and you are already ahead of most players.

BUILD: the five stones of a plan

B
Best square for my worst piece. Find the piece doing least. Give it a job.
U
Unblock. Is one of my pieces trapped behind my own pawns? Open its path.
I
Improve outposts. A knight on a square the enemy pawns cannot attack, supported by my own pawn, is a fortress in his land.
L
Lines. Put rooks on open files, where no pawns stand.
D
Defend the king before attacking. A safe king allows a patient plan.

Patience is a weapon. At this level, a quiet move that improves a piece beats a flashy move that hopes. Ask: "What would my opponent love to do? Can I improve while he cannot?"

Why this is character training

The Pandavas were handed a wilderness and asked to rule it. Patience is the willingness to do an unglamorous task, over and over, because you can see what it will become. A plan played for twenty moves is a quiet act of faith.

3 · Train

20 min

Drill A · The outpost 5 min

White to move. Where does the knight want to live? Check: can any black pawn ever attack that square?

Reveal the answer

Nd5. The e4 pawn supports it, and Black has no c-pawn or e-pawn to chase it away. A knight on d5 is a permanent thorn: it eyes b6, c7, e7 and f6.

Test for an outpost: supported by my pawn, unreachable by his pawns.

Drill B · The buried piece 5 min

White to move. Run BUILD. Which piece is doing the least? Why can it not move?

Reveal the answer

The bishop on c1 is shut in by its own pawns on b2 and d2. It has no legal move at all.

Fix it: d3 or d4 opens the c1–h6 diagonal; or b3 followed by Bb2 uses the other. A free bishop is worth a plan.

Drill C · Audit your own army 10 min

Open one of your own recent games at around move 15. Rate each piece's activity from 1 (buried) to 5 (excellent). Be honest.

4 · Play

20 min

A plan played with patience is stronger than a trick played with hope.

Play one game (15+10 or slower). At moves 8, 14, 20 and 26, stop and run BUILD. Write the plan sentence in your head, then act on it for the next few moves.

Your style's building challenge

Make one preparatory move before you attack. Improve your worst piece first. Your attack will hit harder with every piece in the game.

Give your plan one ambition. Safety is part of a plan, but not all of it. Choose one line, file or outpost to claim.

Time-box your planning. Allow yourself a full minute for the plan at moves 8, 14, 20 and 26, and ten seconds elsewhere. The plan lives in the checkpoint, not in every move.

Choose a plan over a trap. Pick one improvement you would make even if your opponent played perfectly. Let the traps be a bonus.

5 · Reflect

7 min

Greatness is mostly small things done in the right order.

Adapt this lesson to your age
Young wizard (6+)
Play "Find the sleepy piece." Point to the piece that has not moved and ask where it would like to go. Celebrate each piece that wakes up.
Seasoned wizard (60+)
Plans suit experience: you already know how to build. Keep a paper list of the five BUILD letters on the table. A slower game gives time to think.
