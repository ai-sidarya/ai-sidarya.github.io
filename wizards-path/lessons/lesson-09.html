The Yaksha's Questions

When the clock is loud, answer first and drink after.

From the Mahabharata · The Yaksha's questions

During their years in exile, the Pandavas grew desperately thirsty. Nakula went ahead to a clear lake. A voice warned him: "Answer my questions before you drink." He was too thirsty to listen, drank, and fell. One by one, Sahadeva, Arjuna and Bhima did the same.

Last came Yudhishthira. He stopped, listened and answered each question calmly. One was: "What is swifter than the wind?" He replied: "The mind." The voice, who was the god Dharma in disguise, was pleased and gave all four brothers their lives back.

The chess lesson: the clock is your thirst. It pushes you to move before you have thought. Pause, answer first, and drink after.

1 · Centre

3 min

Haste is thirst. Composure is a cup you fill yourself.

  1. Three slow breaths. Slow the exhale.
  2. Feel a moment of urgency in your body. Notice where it lives. Let it pass without acting.
  3. Say: "I answer first, then I move."

2 · The Idea

10 min

Time is the thirst of chess. When the clock runs, your body wants to move at once. The Yaksha's lesson: stop, listen, answer, then drink. A game is decided not only by what you know but by how you spend your minutes.

The traffic light

LightType of moveTime
GreenQuiet, familiar, nothing changed10–15 seconds. Run the Pause and move on.
YellowA real decision between two plans30–90 seconds. Use the Calculation Ladder.
RedCaptures, king in danger, big change in the positionTake your time. This is where games are decided.

Three rules for the clock

  1. Be ahead of the clock at move 15. If you are behind, play Green faster, not Red worse.
  2. A free gift is a question. When something looks free, say: "Why is it free?" The Yaksha asks before you drink.
  3. Safe Mode under two minutes. Play simple moves, keep every piece defended, use forcing moves, and breathe. Increments are your friend.

Composure is not slowness. It is steadiness: the same calm at five minutes as at fifty.

Your style's clock challenge

Your trap is speed. You move fast because attacks feel urgent. Make every Red moment a full stop.

Your trap is over-checking. You verify so much that you run low on time. Trust a clean Scan on Green moves and spend your time on Red.

Your trap is deep thinking everywhere. Give plans a time budget. If you pass a minute on a quiet move, make the move.

Your trap is clock-based swindles. Racing the clock to hope for errors is gambling. Use the clock to keep your own head clear.

5 · Reflect

7 min

The calm mind is faster than the wind.

Adapt this lesson to your age
Young wizard (6+)
Use a sand timer or a kitchen timer and make it a game: "beat the sand." Give a high-five for every pause. Teach one breath: smell the flower, blow the candle.
Seasoned wizard (60+)
Choose a time control with increment so you are never pushed into panic. Quiet moves can go quickly if your Scan is clean. A short walk between games helps.
