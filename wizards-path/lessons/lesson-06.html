Sanjaya's Sight

See the whole board, and tell the truth about what you see.

From the Mahabharata · Sanjaya's sight

The blind king Dhritarashtra longed to know how the great war was going. The sage Vyasa offered to give him sight, but the king could not bear to see the slaughter of his own sons. So Vyasa gave the gift to his minister, Sanjaya.

From the palace, Sanjaya saw the entire battlefield: every chariot, every fallen warrior, every turning point. He reported it all, even the painful parts, never bending the truth to comfort the king.

The chess lesson: see the whole board, and report it as it is, not as you wish it to be. Today your eyes become Sanjaya's.

1 · Centre

3 min

Sight is not what you look at. It is what you are willing to see.

  1. Three slow breaths. Relax your eyes and let them soften.
  2. Look at the room around you. Name three things you had not noticed before.
  3. Say: "I will report what is there, not what I hope for."

2 · The Idea

10 min

Most blunders are not failures of intelligence. They are failures of sight. Sanjaya's gift was seeing everything and reporting it truthfully. Train the same two habits: see it all, then say it honestly.

1 · The Report

Before each move, give a three-line report to yourself, out loud or in a whisper:

Loose
Which pieces are undefended, mine and theirs? Loose pieces drop off.
Lines
Which pieces are pinned or lined up on a file, rank or diagonal?
Light
What is the very next forcing move: a check, a capture or a threat?

2 · Two patterns that win material

PatternWhat you seeWhat you do
PinA piece cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece (or the king) behind it.Attack the pinned piece again, especially with a pawn. It cannot run.
Discovered attackOne of your pieces stands in front of another of your pieces on a line.Move the front piece with a threat, ideally a check, and the piece behind attacks too.

3 · Honest seeing

The enemy of sight is wishful thinking: "He probably won't see it." A true report says, "If he plays the best move, what happens?" Sanjaya never softened a painful scene for the king, and the king was better informed for it. Seeing clearly is a form of courage you can practise on every move.

The 10-second rule. The Report takes about ten seconds on a quiet move. Do it every move for a week and your eye changes for good.

3 · Train

20 min

Drill A · The pin 4 min

White to move. Give the Report. Which black piece cannot move, and why? What attacks it next?

Reveal the answer

d5! The knight on c6 is pinned to the king by the bishop on b5, so it cannot legally move. The pawn now attacks it, and Black cannot save it. If the king steps aside, dxc6 wins the piece.

Lesson: attack the pinned piece, then collect it.

Drill B · The discovered attack 4 min

White to move. The knight stands in front of the rook on the e-file. What happens if the knight moves with a capture?

Reveal the answer

Nxd7+! The knight takes the queen, and moving it uncovers the rook's check on the king. Black must answer the check, so the queen is lost for a knight.

Lesson: a check is a move the opponent cannot ignore. Use it to win something else.

Drill C · Sanjaya's report 7 min

White to move. Give the full Report before you touch a piece. List every loose piece (not pawns, not kings) for both sides. Then find a free gain.

Reveal the answer

Loose: White's rook on a1. Black's bishop on c5.

Free gain: dxc5. The bishop is loose and attacked by the d4 pawn. If Black plays ...Bxf3 first, answer Bxf3 and you are a clean bishop up.

Notice what the report found: not a brilliant sacrifice, just the truth about the position.

Drill D · See without a board 5 min

Close your eyes or look away from any board. Answer each, then reveal.

  1. Is e4 a light or a dark square?
  2. A bishop stands on c1 with nothing in the way. Name the squares on its long diagonal.
  3. Name every square a knight on d4 can reach.
  4. A knight on g1. Where can it go on its first move?
Reveal the answers
  1. Light. (Add the file number to the rank: an even total is dark, an odd one is light. e = 5, so 5 + 4 = 9, light.)
  2. d2, e3, f4, g5, h6.
  3. b3, b5, c2, c6, e2, e6, f3, f5.
  4. e2, f3, h3.

4 · Play

20 min

Whisper the Report, then move.

Play one game (15+10 or slower). On every move, whisper or think the three-line Report: Loose, Lines, Light. Keep two honest counters:

Reports
0
Surprises
0

A surprise is not a failure. It is the exact spot where your sight needs training.

Your style's sight challenge

Report your own loose pieces first. Your eyes go to the enemy king. Count what is hanging in your own camp before you look at his.

Add one hunting question. After you confirm your pieces are safe, ask: "Which enemy piece is loose or pinned?" Safety is the start of the report, not the end.

Tactics before the plan. Your Report comes first. Only when Loose, Lines and Light are clear do you return to the long plan.

Report the best reply, not the hoped one. Say aloud what the opponent plays if he finds the strongest move. If your idea still works, play it.

5 · Reflect

7 min

To see truly is to be free of fear. What is named can be handled.

Adapt this lesson to your age
Young wizard (6+)
Play "I spy" on the board: find every piece nobody protects. Draw a circle around each loose piece with a pencil on a printed diagram. Praise noticing, not winning.
Seasoned wizard (60+)
Keep the three Report words on a card by the board. A larger board, good light and a slower time control make seeing easier. Seeing well is a skill, not a gift.
