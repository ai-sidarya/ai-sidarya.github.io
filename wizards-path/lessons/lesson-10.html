Karma Without Clinging

Do the work with your whole heart. Let the result be the result.

From the Mahabharata · Krishna's teaching on action

After Arjuna's doubt on the field of Kurukshetra, Krishna taught him a way of acting that would free him from fear. "Your right is to the action alone," he said, "never to its fruits." Do your duty with full attention, and let go of your grip on the outcome.

Arjuna picked up his bow again. He did not stop caring. He stopped clinging.

The chess lesson: a rating is a fruit. A game, a puzzle and a review are actions. Give the actions everything you have, and let the fruit ripen in its time.

1 · Centre

3 min

A flower does not hurry itself, and it does not stop blooming.

  1. Three slow breaths.
  2. Name one result you have been gripping too tightly (a rating, a win, a title).
  3. Say: "I give my full effort to the action. I loosen my hold on the result."

2 · The Idea

10 min

You now have nine tools: self-knowledge, the Pause, an opening identity, courage, resilience, sight, patience, humility and composure. Tools do nothing unless used. The final lesson of this part is about rhythm: how to keep going, week after week.

Process beats outcome

Outcome goal (you do not control it)Process goal (you do)
Reach a higher ratingPlay three slow games a week and run the Pause on every move.
Win the tournamentReview one loss each week using the Four Questions.
Never blunderDo ten minutes of puzzles every day.

Krishna's teaching to Arjuna was exactly this: focus on the action, release the outcome. Result goals create anxiety. Process goals create progress, and progress creates results.

The wizard's week

  • Two play days. Slow games, full attention.
  • Two train days. Puzzles, endgames, one lesson from this path.
  • One review day. Review a loss. Update your Board Self card.
  • One rest day. Rest is training.
  • One flex day. Whatever your week needs.

Never miss twice. Missing one day is life. Missing two starts a new habit. A 15-minute session counts. A day of five minutes counts too.

4 · Play

20 min

Play the game for the game's sake. The rating will follow.

Play one slow game (15+10 or slower). Before it begins, write your three process goals on a card. Do not look at your rating before or after. Afterwards, score yourself on the actions only.

The thirty-day circle

Tick a day when you complete even 15 minutes. Come back and keep ticking. Missing a day is allowed; missing two is the thing to avoid.

Your style's rhythm challenge

Schedule your rest. You sprint. Plan the rest day as firmly as the play days. Rest keeps the fire for the long road.

Schedule one stretch day. Once a week, play a game with the opening or plan you usually avoid. Growth lives just outside comfort.

Keep the plan light. A week with seven perfect details will not survive. Choose three process goals and review every Sunday.

Add one fixed anchor. You thrive on variety, so choose a fixed time for one training session each week and keep it.

Adapt this lesson to your age
Young wizard (6+)
Make a sticker chart for the thirty-day circle. Let the child choose the reward for the tenth, twentieth and thirtieth days. Praise showing up, never the result.
Seasoned wizard (60+)
Keep the week light and regular. Link chess to something you already do, such as morning coffee, so the habit has an anchor. Rest days are part of the plan.
