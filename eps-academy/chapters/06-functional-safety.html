Chapter 06: Functional safety and ASIL D

Source statusWritten from practitioner knowledge. Not yet verified line by line against primary sources; confirm standards details before quoting them to a panel.

1. The core idea Core

Functional safety is the discipline of preventing unreasonable risk from malfunctioning electronic behavior. ISO 26262 is the automotive standard. It does not tell you how to design EPS; it tells you how to show that you identified the hazards, built mechanisms against them, and verified the result.

Item definition→Hazard analysis (HARA)→Safety goals→Safety concept→Requirements→Verification→Safety case

2. How ASIL is assigned Level 2

For each hazardous event, rate severity (how bad the harm), exposure (how often the situation occurs) and controllability (can the driver avoid harm). The combination gives QM (no special measures) or ASIL A through D, with D the most stringent. The ratings come from the project's analysis; no function has a built-in ASIL.

Unintended assist at highway speedHighHighHard to controlOften discussed as the highest class; the project's HARA decides
Loss of assist in parkingLowModerateEasyMuch lower class
Do not overclaimNever say "everything in EPS is ASIL D." The label applies to a safety goal and flows to the requirements that implement it. Some software is QM, and mixed-criticality code needs freedom from interference: proof that lower-class code cannot corrupt higher-class code.

3. Safety goals to mechanisms Level 3

Safety goal (example): the system shall not apply unintended assist torque above a threshold. Safe state is the condition with acceptable risk after a fault, for EPS usually a controlled reduction or removal of assist. A fault tolerant time interval is how long a fault may exist before it must be handled. A safety mechanism detects or controls faults, for example an independent monitor that compares commanded and measured motor torque and trips shutdown within the time interval. Hardware faults that would stay hidden are latent faults, which is why self-tests exist.

ASIL decomposition splits a requirement across independent elements of lower class. It only works if the elements are truly independent, which is tested by dependent-failure analysis. Treat claimed decomposition with skepticism until independence is shown.

4. Evidence and who owns it Level 2

ArtifactPurpose
Safety planWhat activities and roles this project commits to
Safety analyses (FMEA, FTA, DFA)Show how faults lead to hazards
Verification resultsProve requirements are met
Confirmation reviews, audit, assessmentIndependent checks of the work
Safety caseThe argument that the product is acceptably safe, pointing to evidence

The safety manager owns the safety argument. The project leader owns planning the work and its resources, and escalates when evidence is missing. Tools used in development need confidence (classification, and qualification where needed) because a faulty compiler or test tool can invalidate evidence.

5. Release with missing evidence Level 4

You cannot declare safety evidence complete when it is not. Your options: reduce scope to what is covered, stage the release (for example a limited vehicle program), or ship with a documented deviation and residual-risk acceptance signed by the safety manager and the customer. What you must not do is pressure the evidence. Tell the customer early, with a date range for the missing items.

Agile and safety: they coexist if safety activities run every iteration (analysis updates, traceability, change-impact), not in a final phase. Fast delivery does not remove evidence; it requires automating the collection.

6. Interview use Level 3

Weak"We follow ISO 26262 and everything is ASIL D, so we test a lot."
Strong"Classification sits on each safety goal. For unintended assist I would ask for the HARA rationale, the detection time against the fault tolerant interval, and independent evidence that the monitor works. If any is missing I change scope or plan, not the evidence."
What the interviewer testsWhether you protect safety under date pressure and whether you can name evidence, not slogans.

7. Self-check and exercise

Exercise: Two weeks before the customer milestone, the independence analysis for a decomposed requirement is incomplete. Notes: do not release as ASIL-decomposed; ask the safety manager for the minimum evidence, offer the customer a staged scope, communicate a date range, and record the decision.
