Chapter 09: Agile, SAFe and lean flow

Source statusPractitioner knowledge. Not yet checked line by line against primary sources. Verify before quoting to a panel.
TakeawayDates slip in queues, not in effort. Fix the system, not the people.

1. Agile in safety-critical work Core

Agile means short feedback loops. It does not mean less evidence. Safety, security and traceability work run every iteration. Not at the end.

Definition of Done must include evidence: tests passed, trace links updated, change impact reviewed. If Done skips evidence, you are deferring risk to the release.

2. Why team Scrum is not enough Level 2

Scrum works inside one team. Steering software has many teams, shared platform code, hardware samples and customer gates. Most delays sit between teams. That is what SAFe tries to manage.

3. SAFe in one page Level 2

TermWhat it is
Agile Release Train (ART)A standing group of teams that plan and deliver together
Program Increment (PI)A planning period, typically 8 to 12 weeks
PI PlanningShared planning event where teams commit to objectives and expose dependencies
EnablerWork that builds architecture, tooling or compliance rather than customer features
Architectural runwayEnablers delivered ahead of the features that need them
Release Train EngineerFacilitates the train: cadence, risks, dependencies
Inspect and AdaptEnd-of-PI demo and problem-solving workshop
ROAMRisk handling: Resolved, Owned, Accepted, Mitigated

Hardware and software run on different cadences. Hardware samples arrive on fixed dates. Plan software integration around them, not around sprint boundaries.

4. Flow: where time actually goes Level 3

  • WIP: work started but not finished. More WIP means slower finishing.
  • Flow efficiency: time someone works on an item divided by total elapsed time. Often below 20%. The rest is waiting.
  • Decision latency: time a decision waits for an owner.
  • Dependency age: how long a cross-team request has been open.
  • Batch size: big releases hide defects and delay feedback.
  • Cost of delay: what one week of delay costs. Use it to rank work.
Why 100% utilization causes slipsWhen people are fully loaded, any surprise queues behind existing work. Waiting time grows sharply as load nears 100%. Keep deliberate slack for defects and customer changes.

5. Fixed date, variable scope Level 3

For a customer gate, fix the date and quality bar. Flex scope. Rank features by customer value and cost of delay, and agree in advance which ones drop. Never flex safety evidence.

Velocity is a planning aid for one team. Never use it to compare teams or rate people. Teams will inflate it, and trust will go.

6. Interview use

Weak"We run SAFe and hold PI planning, so we deliver on time."
Strong"Ceremonies do not create agility. I look at dependency age, WIP and decision latency. Those predict slips before the burndown does."
What the interviewer testsWhether you treat SAFe as a tool and can diagnose delay with flow data.

7. Self-check and exercise

Exercise: Teams show 95% utilization and keep missing PI objectives. Notes: map one feature from idea to release and mark waiting time. Check WIP, dependency age and decision latency. Cut WIP, add slack, assign owners to old dependencies, then re-measure.
