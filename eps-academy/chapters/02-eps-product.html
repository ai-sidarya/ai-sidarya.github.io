Chapter 02: Electronic power steering fundamentals

Source statusWritten from practitioner knowledge. Not yet verified line by line against primary sources; confirm standards details before quoting them to a panel.
One-page cheat sheetEPS replaces hydraulic assist with an electric motor. Driver torque and vehicle speed go into an assist map; the output is a motor torque, delivered through closed-loop current control. Safety hazards are wrong assist (unintended, reversed, oscillating) and lost assist. Software leaders must know the signal chain, the degraded modes and where evidence comes from, not the control math. Print this page for the cheat sheet.

1. What steering must do Core

Steering turns driver intent into wheel angle, gives the driver feedback about the road, and stays controllable at every speed, even when something fails. Power assist exists because parking a heavy car with no assist takes high effort, but too much assist at speed feels vague and unsafe. So assist must vary with speed.

2. Four generations of assist Core

TypeHow it worksTrade-off
ManualMechanical link onlyNo assist, heavy at low speed
Hydraulic (HPS)Engine-driven pump, fluid pressure assistsPump drags the engine even when not steering
Electro-hydraulic (EHPS)Electric motor drives the pumpStill hydraulics, complex
Electric (EPS)Motor applies torque directly, controlled by softwareEfficient and tunable; software and safety now dominate

EPS is categorized by where the motor acts: column (C-EPS), pinion (P-EPS), dual pinion (DP-EPS) and rack (R-EPS). Heavier vehicles and higher loads tend to use motors closer to the rack. Treat this as a conceptual rule; applications vary.

3. Signal flow Level 2

Driver torque sensor+Vehicle speed (CAN)→Assist map→Torque command→Current controller→Inverter PWM→Motor→Rack

Inputs: torque across a torsion bar, steering angle, vehicle speed, motor rotor position, phase currents, supply voltage and temperature. The assist map converts driver torque and speed into a requested assist torque. An inner loop controls motor current fast (typically thousands of times per second) so delivered torque follows the request. Sensor signals are conditioned (filtered, scaled) and checked for plausibility, for example a redundant torque signal must agree with its twin.

4. What makes it feel like a car Level 2

FunctionPurpose
Returnability / active returnWheel returns to center after a turn
DampingPrevents oscillation and kickback
Friction compensationHides mechanical friction for smooth feel
Pull-drift compensationCorrects constant pull from road camber or alignment

Steering feel is subjective, tuned by calibration: parameter sets that differ by vehicle. This is why customer acceptance of feel is a delivery risk, not just an engineering one.

5. Failure and degraded modes Level 3

FailureEffectTypical response
Loss of assistSteering becomes heavy; mechanical link remainsWarn driver, controlled fade-out
Unintended assistWheel pulls without driver inputDetect fast, shut down or limit
OscillationWheel shakesDamping, limit, shut down
Sensor or communication faultBad input to the mapPlausibility check, fallback signal, degrade
Thermal or low voltageLimited motor capabilityReduce assist gradually, warn
Why abrupt shutdown is a riskDropping assist suddenly at speed or mid-corner can itself surprise the driver. Degraded behavior is designed to fade, which is why it needs vehicle-level validation, not only bench tests.

6. Why it matters to a manager

Wrong assist is generally treated as the most severe failure class, so safety mechanisms, timing and evidence dominate schedule. Calibration and vehicle integration consume the end of the program. Interfaces (CAN speed signal, power supply, vehicle modes) are owned by the OEM, so late signal changes become customer escalations.

8. Interview use Level 3

Weak"EPS uses a motor and software to help the driver steer, and safety is very important."
Strong"The key hazards are wrong assist and lost assist. Wrong assist is more severe because it acts against driver intent, so detection time and safe-state behavior drive the software design and the evidence I would want before release."
What the interviewer testsWhether you can describe the failure modes that justify the process, not whether you can recite the architecture.

9. Self-check and exercise

Exercise: Two customers want different steering feel for the same platform software. How do you decide what is calibration and what is a software change? Notes: keep behavior differences in parameters where possible, treat each software change as a safety-impact question, and ask the architect and safety manager before promising it.
